# Module 7: The Portfolio and the Job Market

This module covers the work of turning a semester of course projects into a portfolio that an employer can open and inspect, and it describes what the analytics job market now expects of graduates who work with AI tools. A portfolio in this sense is a small set of finished artifacts (e.g., a code repository, a written analysis, or a short recorded demonstration) that a hiring manager can evaluate without a meeting. Employers who screen analytics candidates may ask how a candidate used an AI assistant on a problem, not only what the final numbers were, which makes the record of the work as valuable as the result. The market itself moves quickly, so a graduate who follows the field can speak about the tools that are current rather than about the tools that were current when the degree began. We treat the portfolio and the job market as a single topic because the portfolio is the artifact through which a student answers the questions the market asks.

A student who completes this module should be able to select two or three course projects that show a range of skills and prepare each one for outside inspection. Preparing a project means writing a short summary of the question, the data, and the decisions made along the way, including the points at which an AI tool was used and the points at which its output was checked or rejected. A student should also be able to describe, in an interview, a specific workflow in which an assistant carried part of the work, and to say plainly what was verified by hand. A third skill is the habit of following the field, since the vocabulary of the market changes faster than a curriculum can, and a candidate who uses last year's terms sounds further from the work than the candidate actually is. Thus, the practical output of this module is a short portfolio together with a reading and listening routine that keeps it current.
