# People and Newsletters

The entries below are individuals whose writing, teaching, or newsletters we follow because they explain how the current generation of AI tools works rather than merely announcing what was released. Each entry links to the person's own site or newsletter (never to a profile on a third-party platform), names what the person publishes, and records the date on which we last confirmed the description.

<!-- resources: type=person -->
### [Allie K. Miller](https://www.alliekmiller.com/)

**Person**, Allie K. Miller. A former leader of Amazon Web Services' Global Machine Learning for Startups and Venture Capital organization who now advises and trains Fortune 500 companies, and who offers The AI Fast Track, a free five-day email course taught using Claude. Featured in [Module 5](../path/module-05.md), [Module 7](../path/module-07.md). *Last verified 2026-09-07.*

### [Ethan Mollick (One Useful Thing)](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/)

**Person**, Ethan Mollick. A Wharton professor writes this research-based newsletter on the implications of AI for work, education, and life, drafting every post himself and pointing readers to free AI resources and prompts compiled at Wharton's Generative AI Labs. Featured in [Module 7](../path/module-07.md). *Last verified 2026-09-07.*

### [Matt Pocock (AI Hero)](https://www.aihero.dev/)

**Person**, Matt Pocock. Tutorials and courses on working effectively with AI coding agents, i.e., tools that read, write, and run code on a developer's behalf, including how to build skills for those agents. Featured in [Module 2](../path/module-02.md), [Module 4](../path/module-04.md). *Last verified 2026-09-07.*

### [Sebastian Raschka (Ahead of AI)](https://sebastianraschka.com/)

**Person**, Sebastian Raschka. A newsletter on machine learning and AI research read by more than 200,000 researchers and practitioners, written by the author of the book Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch). Featured in [Module 6](../path/module-06.md). *Last verified 2026-09-07.*

### [Simon Willison's Weblog](https://simonwillison.net/)

**Person**, Simon Willison. A weblog covering programming and web development since 2002, with a newsletter on AI, large language models (LLMs), data science, SQLite, and Python, written without compensation for covering particular topics. Featured in [Module 6](../path/module-06.md). *Last verified 2026-09-07.*
